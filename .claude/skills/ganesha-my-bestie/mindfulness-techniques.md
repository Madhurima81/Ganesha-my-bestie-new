# Mindfulness Techniques — Ganesha My Bestie

## Core Sources

- **Relax Kids** — Marneta Viegas (25 years, 5M+ children, 52 countries)
- **Inner Kids** — Susan Kaiser Greenland (UCLA Mindful Awareness Research Center)
- **MindUP** — Goldie Hawn Foundation (CASEL SELect accredited)
- **Mindful Kids** — Ciaran Ivanovic (N.A.P. strategy)
- **Peace Out** — Chanel Tsang (guided imagery + breathing stories)
- **Zones of Regulation** — Leah Kuypers
- Clinical: Cedars-Sinai Children's Health, UCLA, OT Toolbox, UC Berkeley

---

## Relax Kids 7-Step System

**Move → Play → Stretch → Feel → Breathe → Believe → Relax**

Start where the child IS energetically. High energy first, travel to calm.
Never say "calm down" — always *travel* to calm.
The child is a participant, not an audience.

GMB Application: Ganesha meets the child in their zone first, then journeys with them.

---

## N.A.P. Strategy (Mindful Kids)

**Notice → Accept → Plan**

Child pauses → notices feeling without judgment → makes a positive plan.
Works for ages 5–12. Ganesha can guide this as a 3-step journey.

## ⚠️ Contraindications — Never Violate These

```
❌ NEVER scribble art for Blue zone — can amplify sadness
❌ NEVER silly dance for ages 10–12 — feels babyish, breaks trust
❌ NEVER 5-4-3-2-1 for ages 5–7 — too cognitively complex
❌ NEVER hold breath with trauma-affected children
❌ NEVER rush through a technique — Ganesha is unhurried always
❌ NEVER skip the "Notice" step — always name the zone first
```

## Technique Selection Algorithm

```
1. Identify child's zone (Red/Yellow/Blue/Green)
2. Note age group (5–7 / 8–10 / 11–12)
3. Check cooldown — has this been used in last N sessions?
4. Select 3 options:
   → 1 body-based
   → 1 breathing
   → 1 mindfulness/creative
5. Let child choose — agency is part of regulation
6. After technique: brief reflection → "How do you feel now?"
7. Log technique + zone + response to parent dashboard
```

## Peace Out Style — Guided Story Integration

Weave technique INTO a Ganesha story. Child is participant not audience.
Example: "Can you feel your feet on the ground right now? That's exactly how Ganesha felt when he stood on his mountain before writing the Mahabharata."

Every guided story should include:
- 1 breathing moment
- 1 sensory anchor ("what can you feel/smell/hear?")
- 1 empowerment moment (child does something alongside Ganesha)
