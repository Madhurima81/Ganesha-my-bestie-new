## 2. Collision Rules

- **V2-02 vs V2-07**: Choose V2-07 only when a shrinking window, scarce chance, or emotional margin drives the final move. Choose V2-02 when the same old reaction pattern is what keeps recurring and must be interrupted.
- **V2-02 vs V2-11**: Choose V2-11 when trapped attention on one thing is the actual problem. Choose V2-02 when the problem is a recurring reaction loop under pressure.
- **V2-02 vs V2-12**: Choose V2-12 when over-effort or force is insufficient and quiet capacity solves. Choose V2-02 when recurrence plus interruption is still the core engine.
- **V2-04 vs V2-09**: Choose V2-04 when an assumption is overturned by an unexpected contributor. Choose V2-09 when inadequate A and B choices must fail before reflection yields C.
- **V2-04 vs V2-12**: Choose V2-04 when resolution comes from what was dismissed outside the protagonist. Choose V2-12 when resolution comes from a quieter capacity already inside the protagonist.
- **V2-07 vs V2-12**: Choose V2-07 when urgency/scarcity is doing the causal work. Choose V2-12 when the real turn is from pushing/intensity to calm, pacing, or grounding.
- **V2-09 vs V2-10**: Choose V2-10 when two live paths compete at one decisive threshold. Choose V2-09 when the story needs failed first and second solutions before the truer third move.
- **V2-11 vs V2-12**: Choose V2-11 when fixation on one thing narrows meaning until it widens. Choose V2-12 when quiet regulation or steadiness is the causal answer to insufficient force/effort.
- **V2-01 vs V2-05**: Choose V2-01 when multiple fragments accumulate into pattern recognition. Choose V2-05 when each clue actively creates the next question.
- **V2-03 vs V2-06**: Choose V2-03 when mirrored opening/ending scenes prove the change. Choose V2-06 when receive-help then give-help symmetry is essential.
