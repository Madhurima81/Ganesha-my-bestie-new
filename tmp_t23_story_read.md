## SIT042 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** Bodhi asks a friend to play, but the friend says they don't want to play today. Bodhi asks, the way Bodhi always asks. 

**Page 2:** At first Bodhi was still listening to the thought that If my friend chooses someone else, I'm not important. Their friend said, "Not today" — no reason given. 

**Page 3:** Their friend still waved before running off, the same wave as any other day. Bodhi went quiet for a second — a "no" today didn't erase every "yes" before it. 

**Page 4:** Bodhi said, "Okay — maybe tomorrow?" instead of walking away certain something was over. 

**Page 5:** Bodhi found something else to do, still counting on tomorrow.


**Compression:** Bodhi asks a friend to play, but the friend says they don't want to play today, and the old thought said If my friend chooses someone else, I'm not important. The same wave came anyway, before Bodhi's friend ran off, same as any other day. Bodhi asked for tomorrow instead of writing the friendship off.

## SIT086 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** Tara has exciting news they have been saving to tell a parent, but the parent starts an important work call. Tara has been holding the news inside all afternoon. 

**Page 2:** At first Tara was still listening to the thought that If I have to wait, what I want to say isn't important. The phone rang, and Their parent could not listen right now. 

**Page 3:** Mid-call, Their parent still caught Tara's eye and said, "One minute.". Tara realized the wait had an end — it just hadn't arrived yet. 

**Page 4:** Tara waited for a second, then went and did something else instead of watching the clock. 

**Page 5:** The moment the call ended, Their parent turned straight to Tara, just as glad to hear it now.


**Compression:** Tara has exciting news they have been saving to tell a parent, but the parent starts an important work call, and the old thought said If I have to wait, what I want to say isn't important. Mid-call, Tara still got a caught eye and a mouthed "one minute". Tara let the call end instead of standing there watching the clock.

## SIT089 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** Bodhi has something they want to show the family, but everyone is gathered around the new baby. Bodhi stands beside the sofa holding a picture they made. 

**Page 2:** At first Bodhi was still listening to the thought that The new baby has replaced me. Their mom was busy with the new baby, and nobody looked up yet. 

**Page 3:** Their mom turned the camera toward Bodhi too, without being asked. Something in Bodhi loosened — there was room enough for the baby and for Bodhi, both at once. 

**Page 4:** Bodhi waited for a second, then held the picture up instead of quietly putting it away. 

**Page 5:** Their mom said, "Come sit with us," and Bodhi kept the picture out, part of the same photo as the baby.


**Compression:** Bodhi has something they want to show the family, but everyone is gathered around the new baby, and the old thought said The new baby has replaced me. The camera turned toward Bodhi too, without anyone having to ask. Bodhi held the picture up and ended up in the same photo as the baby.

## SIT123 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** Bodhi notices that a close friend has started talking, behaving, or dressing differently around other children. Yesterday, Bodhi's friend laughed at the same silly joke. 

**Page 2:** At first Bodhi was still listening to the thought that I have to change to keep my friends. Their friend started using new words and a new style, like a whole new person overnight. 

**Page 3:** Their friend still sat right next to Bodhi at lunch, same seat as always. Bodhi went quiet for a second, because the seat next to them hadn't actually moved, whatever the new words sounded like. 

**Page 4:** Bodhi dropped the copied voice and said, "Nice one, right?" — the same joke, told the old way. 

**Page 5:** Bodhi kept talking the way Bodhi always had, whether or not it was the "cool" way anymore.


**Compression:** Bodhi notices that a close friend has started talking, behaving, or dressing differently around other children, and the old thought said I have to change to keep my friends. Bodhi's friend still sat right there at lunch, same seat as always, cooler words and all. Bodhi dropped the copied voice and told the old joke the way that was actually theirs.

## SIT158 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** A friend who hurt Bodhi's feelings comes back and apologises. The friend stands beside Bodhi quietly. 

**Page 2:** At first Bodhi was still listening to the thought that Holding on to hurt keeps me safe. Their friend said, "I'm sorry" — the exact words Bodhi had been waiting for. 

**Page 3:** The hurt was still sitting there in Bodhi's chest, even after hearing it. Bodhi sat with that for a second, because still feeling hurt and accepting the apology were not opposites. 

**Page 4:** Bodhi said, "Thanks — I'm still sad, but I'm glad you said it.". 

**Page 5:** Bodhi kept sitting right there with their friend anyway, hurt and okay at the same time.


**Compression:** A friend who hurt Bodhi's feelings comes back and apologises, and the old thought said Holding on to hurt keeps me safe. The hurt stayed put in the middle of the chest, right there even after the apology came. Bodhi accepted the apology anyway, without pretending the hurt was already gone.

## SIT133 (locked=true)

**Page 1:** Simha is with friends at a shop or another child's house, and a friend dares Simha to slip something small into their pocket without paying or asking. 'Come on, nobody's looking. 

**Page 2:** At first Simha was still listening to the thought that I must follow my friends to belong. Simha's hand drifted toward it, half-following before deciding anything. 

**Page 3:** Simha's hand stopped on its own, hovering, not moving any closer.
Simha realized nobody had actually said it, so that part was only ever in Simha's own head. 

**Page 4:** Simha said, "Nah, I'm good," and stepped back. 

**Page 5:** Simha walked away with empty pockets, still right there with the others — belonging never required taking it in the first place.


**Compression:** Simha is with friends at a shop or another child's house, and a friend dares Simha to slip something small into their pocket without paying or asking, and the old thought said I must follow my friends to belong. Simha's own hand stopped on its own, before any decision was made out loud. Simha said no and stayed part of the group anyway.
