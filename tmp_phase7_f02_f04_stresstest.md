# F02 (Discovery) vs F04 (Connection) — stress test

Testing on three situations deliberately chosen to be unfriendly to easy convergence: no natural "newcomer brings something valuable" pull like SIT164 had.

---

## 1. SIT086 — Parent is busy when you want to share something
*storySeed: Kavi has exciting news, but the parent starts an important work call. Want: tell the parent. Obstacle: parent can't listen mid-call. Tension: waiting feels like being overlooked.*

### F02 — Discovery
1. Kavi retreats from the closed door, news still unshared, and starts pacing near the parent's desk.
2. Something on the desk/shelf catches Kavi's eye — not the parent, an object (an old tin, a strange tool, a photo Kavi's never really looked at).
3. Kavi investigates it directly — opens it, tries it, turns it over.
4. The object leads to a further curiosity — the tin has something inside connected to another mystery, or the tool does something unexpected when tried.
5. Kavi gets pulled fully into figuring out what this thing is/does, waiting time forgotten.
6. **Final discovery:** Kavi solves or understands the object just as the call ends — and now has *two* things to share, not one.

### F04 — Connection
1. Kavi hovers near the parent mid-call, news undelivered, feeling brushed off.
2. Kavi's first response: quiet frustration, maybe a small interruption attempt, gently waved off.
3. **Reveal:** Kavi notices the parent's shoulders are tight, voice tighter than usual — this call isn't just "adult boring stuff," the parent looks stressed.
4. Kavi realizes the parent isn't choosing the call over Kavi — the parent is stuck somewhere uncomfortable too.
5. Kavi changes response: stops trying to interrupt, instead does something small and undemanding (sits quietly nearby, brings a glass of water) instead of pushing.
6. **Connection:** call ends, parent notices Kavi waited *with* them rather than *at* them, and gives full attention — repaired through the noticing, not through the news itself.

| | F02 | F04 |
|---|---|---|
| Drives next event | The object's next unanswered question | The parent's next visible emotional cue |
| Hero is trying to understand | What this thing is / does | What the parent is actually feeling |
| What changes | Kavi's picture of the object; waiting stops being "empty" | Kavi's picture of the parent; being unavailable stops meaning "not caring" |
| Turning point | The object connects to something bigger than expected | Kavi sees the parent's stress instead of the parent's distraction |
| Why the ending happens | The mystery resolves on its own terms | The parent notices *how* Kavi waited |

**Removal test:** Strip the parent from F02 entirely — replace with "Kavi was bored waiting for anything" — the discovery plot is completely unaffected. Strip the object from F04 entirely — the whole plot still runs on Kavi + parent's emotional state alone. **Pass — no accidental convergence.**

## 2. SIT123 — Friend suddenly acting different or "cooler"
*storySeed: a close friend starts talking/dressing/behaving differently. Want: keep the friendship as it was. Obstacle: can't control the friend's choices. Tension: wonders if different means distant.*

### F02 — Discovery
1. Kavi notices the friend using a new phrase, doing a new trick, into a new show — something concrete and specific, not just "acting weird."
2. Curiosity (not hurt) drives the next step: Kavi investigates the *thing itself* — what the phrase means, where the trick came from, what the show is about.
3. Kavi tries it — the new phrase, the trick — and discovers it's genuinely interesting on its own merits, independent of the friend.
4. This opens a bigger discovery: a whole scene/fandom/skill-world Kavi didn't know existed.
5. Expectation changes: "different" stops meaning a closed door and starts meaning a door into something Kavi didn't know about.
6. **Final discovery:** Kavi gets good enough at the new thing to understand *why* it's exciting — the discovery is complete and meaningful on its own, regardless of what happens with the friendship.

### F04 — Connection
1. Kavi notices the same surface change — new slang, new group, brushing off old jokes.
2. Kavi's first response: tries to force the old friendship back — repeats an old routine, gets a distracted or embarrassed reaction.
3. **Reveal:** Kavi catches the friend looking uneasy around the new group too — not fully confident, maybe even anxious about fitting in.
4. Kavi realizes the friend isn't discarding him — the friend is nervous, and possibly afraid of losing Kavi *while* trying to belong somewhere new.
5. Kavi changes response: stops chasing the old version, offers something that doesn't demand the friend choose — a private joke resurfacing naturally, or just showing up without pressure.
6. **Connection repaired**, shown through the friend relaxing and including Kavi in the new thing too — not through Kavi learning to like the new thing for its own sake.

| | F02 | F04 |
|---|---|---|
| Drives next event | Curiosity about the new thing itself | The friend's next emotional tell |
| Hero is trying to understand | What this new interest actually is | What the friend is feeling underneath the change |
| What changes | Kavi's relationship to the *new thing* | Kavi's understanding of the *friend's* inner state |
| Turning point | The new thing turns out to have real value on its own | Kavi sees the friend's insecurity instead of the friend's rejection |
| Why the ending happens | The discovery is satisfying in itself | The friend responds to being met without pressure |

**Removal test:** Strip the friendship from F02 — "Kavi discovers a new hobby/trend" — fully coherent alone, no friend required to complete the arc. Strip the discovered *thing* from F04 entirely (delete all detail about what the friend is into) — the plot still runs purely on noticing the friend's anxiety and adjusting response. **Pass.** This was the closer call of the three — F02's step 6 draws right up to the line of "and that's why the friend didn't leave Kavi behind," which would collapse it into F04. Kept it out on purpose; the ending stays about the discovered thing's own value, and any friendship read is a side effect, not the plot engine.

## 3. SIT143 — Seeing someone litter
*storySeed: Kavi sees someone drop litter and walk away. Want: do something about it. Obstacle: unsure if it's their place to speak up. Tension: bothered but tempted to stay silent.*

This is the hard case: the person who caused the situation has already left, so neither form gets an obvious "other character" handed to it for free.

### F02 — Discovery
1. Kavi notices the wrapper land near the bench, the person already gone.
2. Instead of only feeling bothered, curiosity catches on the litter itself — Kavi looks closer.
3. Investigating, Kavi finds more than one piece — a small unnoticed trail leading under a bush, more than expected.
4. Kavi discovers *why* it matters concretely — a bird pecking at it wrong, or the bin actually just a few steps away the whole time, meaning this was entirely avoidable.
5. Expectation changes: "one small wrapper" becomes "a pattern I almost walked past."
6. **Final discovery:** the single piece of litter Kavi almost ignored turns out to be one visible piece of something bigger, unnoticed by everyone else who's walked past all day.

**Ending:** Kavi cleans it up / reports it, driven entirely by now understanding the scale of the thing — no other character needed for the ending to land.

### F04 — Connection
Because the litterer is gone, this form has to find its relationship honestly rather than force a confrontation-and-redemption with a stranger who left — that would risk exactly the "fake villain" trap the forms warn against.

1. **Encounter:** Kavi is walking with a younger sibling when they both see the litter get dropped. The sibling shrugs: "so?"
2. Kavi's first response: says nothing, just picks it up alone, privately assuming the sibling doesn't care.
3. **Reveal:** the sibling, watching, admits they'd noticed it too and felt bothered — but didn't know if it was "their place" to do anything, mirroring Kavi's exact hesitation.
4. Kavi realizes the sibling wasn't indifferent — just as unsure as Kavi was a minute ago.
5. Kavi changes response: invites the sibling to help instead of handling it silently and alone.
6. **Connection:** they pick it up together — the resolution is the shared act between Kavi and the sibling, not Kavi's understanding of litter.

| | F02 | F04 |
|---|---|---|
| Drives next event | What's physically found next at the scene | The sibling's next reaction |
| Hero is trying to understand | The scale/cause of the litter problem | Whether the sibling feels the same way Kavi does |
| What changes | Kavi's picture of the *place* | Kavi's picture of the *sibling* |
| Turning point | Discovering it's a pattern, not one wrapper | Discovering the sibling shares Kavi's hesitation |
| Why the ending happens | The scale of the discovery justifies acting | Acting together resolves the shared hesitation |

**Removal test:** Strip every other character from F02 (no sibling, no one) — story is completely unaffected, it was never about a person. Strip the litter's factual detail from F04 (no bird, no bin distance, no "pattern" — just "there was litter") — F04 still works exactly as written, since none of that factual detail ever appeared in it. **Pass — and this is the cleanest pass of the three**, because the litterer being gone strips away any lazy shortcut (there's no "confront and redeem the litterer" option sitting right there), forcing each form to find its own honest engine. F02 had to go all-in on the physical scene; F04 had to invent a real relationship (the sibling) rather than fake one with an absent stranger.

---

## Overall verdict

All three pass the stated condition: **removing the relationship from F02 still leaves a coherent discovery story, and removing the discovery from F04 still leaves a coherent relationship story**, in all three situations, including two (SIT086, SIT143) with no natural pull toward either form and one (SIT123) that came close but held.

The boundary is structural, not just tonal — confirmed by three independent situations rather than the one friendly case. **Recommend locking F01–F05 as specified**, with one carried-forward caution: SIT123-style situations (where the "different" thing has real content of its own, e.g. a hobby, a trend, a skill) are the genuine risk zone for F02/F04 bleed and deserve a specific editorial check at the templates layer later, not just at Form-design time.
